# Beat nomination: direction in, novelty never in the evidence middle

Reporting touches the newsroom's screen-design step at exactly one point,
reading the news for one purpose, and **never** enters the evidence chain. (A
separate, later touchpoint — the post-verification novelty scan on a promoted
case — belongs to `track-investigation`; see its
`reference/prior-art.md`. The two share one firewall: **direction in, novelty
out, news never in the evidence middle.**)

The firewall (*a claim cites only source records*) governs **evidence**, not
**attention**. Reading the news to choose what to investigate never cites an
article as proof of anything in the data. So this is not a firewall exception —
news is simply a second, fenced provenance class: *what has been said about the
world*, kept separate from *what the records show*.

## Direction in (beat nomination)

Use reporting to decide where to point the corpus when designing screens.
Discipline:

- **Toward the beat, not the claim.** Reporting is free signal that a topic has
  *live* influence activity. Aim the corpus's distinctive lens (lobbying filings,
  contribution records, say-vs-pay) at the **layer reporting can't reach** — the
  influence plumbing under the headline. Outlets cover the policy outcome; we
  cover who filed, who paid, who went quiet. Same hot topic → an
  `under-reported` finding by construction.
- **Don't flee crowded topics.** "Away from what's covered" fails twice: crowded
  ≠ exhausted (an un-mined seam remains at our granularity), and empty ≠ fertile
  — *uncrowded often means the data is thin/junk, not that the story is
  undiscovered* (the coverage-gap trap). Satisfy the scoop instinct by going
  **deeper within a hot topic**, not by chasing cold ones.
- **Log nomination as selection context, never as evidence.** "This beat was
  nominated by [coverage]" is recorded as motivation on the screen — the same
  category as "which screen surfaced this, out of how many" in the
  multiple-comparisons ledger. A red-team needs to know a screen was
  reporting-inspired; the reader's evidence is still only the records.
- **Name the anchor in the boring explanation.** A reporting-nominated lead's
  boring explanation (written when a screen it inspires surfaces one) must
  answer *"am I just confirming the article?"* — the cure for provenance
  laundering (importing an external claim and dressing ambiguous rows as a
  primary-record confirmation).
