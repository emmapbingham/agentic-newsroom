## Hypothesis

The JACK Act (2019) requires every lobbyist with a predicate criminal
conviction to have that conviction disclosed on every LDA filing that lists
them — registration and all subsequent quarterlies, forever, no lookback
limit. In this corpus, at least two convicted lobbyists' filings fail this
requirement in different ways, and the law's only enforcement mechanism (the
U.S. Attorney's Office for D.C., audited annually by GAO) has never brought a
single civil or criminal action for a JACK Act violation since the law took
effect — and GAO's own audit methodology is structurally unable to catch
either failure mode documented here. The story is not about one lobbyist's
intent; it's a transparency law failing at its edges with no mechanism
watching for it.

**Two documented failure modes, one enforcement vacuum:**
1. **Hunter/VALOON (Trex Enterprises):** the disclosure was present once — on
   Duncan Hunter's own October 2023 registration, self-filed under his own
   name — then absent from all 6 subsequent quarterlies, also self-filed
   under his own name. No boundary event, no preparer change, no pardon-based
   legal ambiguity his own prior filing didn't already resolve against him.
2. **Hanson/VVA (Vietnam Veterans of America):** the disclosure appears and
   disappears at onboarding/departure boundaries (2023 Q3+Q4, 2025 Q1), and
   even when present, cites the wrong legal instrument (a 2013 administrative
   settlement, not the 2009 criminal conviction that is the actual predicate).
3. **The enforcement layer:** GAO's 2026 compliance audit (GAO-26-108486)
   confirms zero JACK Act prosecutions or civil actions since 2019, and its
   own compliance-check methodology audits a random sample for whether
   lobbyists have convictions at all, rather than auditing known-convicted
   lobbyists' disclosure behavior — so a gap like Hunter's or Hanson's would
   be invisible to the one process meant to catch it. This is not a one-year
   artifact: all 6 prior annual GAO reports (2019-2024 coverage) use the same
   methodology and report the same zero-disclosed-convictions result every
   single year since the law took effect (E-GAO-series). No other external
   body has any role either — no private right of action exists, and
   lobbying disclosure has sat outside House/Senate Ethics Committee
   jurisdiction since 1977-78 (E-enforcement-map). The Clerk of the
   House/Secretary of the Senate do carry a separate, affirmative
   "review each filing for accuracy" duty (2 U.S.C. §1605(a)(2)) distinct
   from the failure-to-file referral pipeline — CRS confirms the duty
   exists but not its practical depth, so the case does not claim this
   duty is toothless, only that no source (CRS, GAO, or the offices'
   own guidance) describes it ever catching a JACK Act disclosure gap
   (E-enforcement-map).

## Why it's newsworthy

A 2019 transparency law was written for exactly this scenario: so Congress
and the public know when a lobbyist with a fraud, false-statement, or
corruption-adjacent conviction is working the halls. Two very different
filers — a pardoned former congressman filing under his own name, and a
veterans' charity's in-house lobbyist — both have gaps in a legally
mandated, zero-lookback disclosure, and no one has ever been fined or
prosecuted under this law. That's not two isolated oversights; it's a
due-diligence requirement running on the honor system, unchecked, with
public evidence it sometimes doesn't hold.

## What would confirm it / what would kill it

- **Confirms:** additional convicted lobbyists in the register showing the
  same pattern (checked — see E-register: 18 total, most compliant, a small
  number of outliers; the story is about the outliers existing and going
  unchecked, not a corpus-wide epidemic); Burkman and Wohl specifically
  checked for undiscovered gaps of their own (see E-corpuspct — Burkman
  0/471 missing, Wohl 1/182 missing, neither is a third gap case; corpus-wide
  1.13% of post-conviction filing-instances lack the disclosure, 20% of the
  15 lobbyists with post-conviction quarterlies have at least one gap
  quarter); the GAO enforcement facts holding up on a full read (done — see
  E-GAO); no legal off-ramp (pardon, predicate-offense ambiguity) that
  cleanly excuses either filer.
- **Refutes / boring:** (a) Hunter's gap is clerical, not evasive — weighed
  against: same self-filer (Duncan Hunter) on the RR and all 6 quarterlies,
  no preparer-change or boundary-event explanation available (unlike
  Hanson) — see EHunter1-4; (b) Hanson's gap is a "flicker" implying ongoing
  evasion — already refuted in the prior drilldown: it's two boundary
  events, not a scattered pattern (kept as this case's Hanson chapter,
  reframed accordingly); (c) GAO's audit shows the system basically works —
  refuted on a close read: GAO found zero convictions in a random sample of
  247 lobbyists, which is a base-rate artifact (predicate convictions are
  rare) and says nothing about whether known-convicted lobbyists like
  Hunter/Hanson are disclosing correctly, since GAO's methodology never
  looks at that population; (d) enforcement numbers are just low because
  violations are rare — partially true but doesn't explain zero, not low, in
  seven years, nor address that the referral system that does exist only
  catches non-filers, not filers who omit one field.

## Verdict

**Supported, high confidence.** Two independently source-verified filers —
Duncan Hunter/VALOON (0 of 6 post-conviction quarterlies disclosed, after
one disclosure on his own registration) and Harold Hanson/VVA (3 of 7
missing, and even the present disclosures cite the wrong legal instrument)
— both have uncured gaps in the JACK Act's zero-lookback disclosure
requirement, with no preparer-change, pardon, or predicate-ambiguity
explanation that survives scrutiny. Independently, GAO's compliance audit
has found zero JACK Act prosecutions or civil actions in the law's entire
2019-2025 history, and its own audit methodology samples random lobbyists
for undisclosed convictions rather than auditing known-convicted lobbyists'
disclosure behavior — so a gap like Hunter's or Hanson's is structurally
invisible to the one process meant to catch it, a pattern that holds across
all 7 annual reports, not a one-year artifact. No other enforcement channel
exists (no private right of action, no Ethics Committee jurisdiction since
1977-78); the Clerk/Secretary's own accuracy-review duty is real but its
practical depth is undocumented in any source found. Checked directly:
corpus-wide, only 1.13% of post-conviction filing-instances lack the
disclosure (877/887), and 3 of 15 lobbyists with post-conviction
quarterlies (20%) have any gap at all — Burkman and Wohl, the corpus's two
high-volume filers, were confirmed not to have hidden gaps of their own
(Burkman 0/471, Wohl 1/182, a boundary-quarter effect). This low rate
*supports* rather than undercuts the claim: the story was never "this is
common," it's "it's rare, real, and nothing is positioned to catch it" —
which is exactly what the corpus-wide check confirms. A builder→skeptic→judge
tribunal (2026-07-07) ran the full corpus-specific checklist (namesake/
identity, House+Senate double-counting, multiple comparisons, base rate,
data-quality applicability) against the combined claim and could not
refute it; full reasoning in `evidence.md` E-tribunal. Novelty-scanned
2026-07-07: no prior coverage found of either filer's gap or the
GAO-methodology argument, in isolation or combined (`novel`, hedged per the
standard bounded-search caveat — see Prior coverage below).

## Prior coverage

Hunter + Hanson + enforcement-vacuum framing, not either filer alone).
**Verdict: novel** (hedged per the asymmetry rule — a miss is weak evidence,
bounded by search recall).

- **The systemic argument (GAO's audit methodology structurally can't catch
  a known-convicted lobbyist's disclosure gap) — no coverage found.** Law-firm
  client alerts (Ballard Spahr, Steptoe, Kelley Drye, Arnold & Porter, Whiteford
  Taylor) all cover the JACK Act's *requirements* at 2019 passage; none
  revisit compliance since. No outlet or watchdog piece found connecting
  GAO's random-sample methodology to a base-rate-artifact critique.
- **Enforcement vacuum — the underlying fact is GAO's own disclosure, not a
  scoop, but nobody has turned it into a story.** Confirms E-GAO from GAO's
  own report text; no journalism found reporting "zero JACK Act
  prosecutions since 2019" as a finding in its own right. One indirect
  corroboration: Arnold & Porter's Enforcement Edge post frames the 2020
  Jack Abramoff case as "the first-ever criminal prosecution... under the
  LDA" (not JACK Act-specific), and independently confirms the zero-JACK-Act-
  prosecutions state of play by omission — no JACK Act case is mentioned
  post-2019 in any source found.
- **Hunter — disclosure-gap pattern: no coverage found.** LegiStorm's piece
  (surfacing-time hit, unchanged) covers the registration, the Trex/earmark
  backstory, and the pardon — not the 6-quarter disclosure gap.
- **Hanson — disclosure-gap pattern: no coverage found.** New this scan: a
  Federal Register notice and OpenSanctions entry both independently confirm
  the BIS order is an *administrative* export-control matter (15-year denial
  order, 2013), not framed as a criminal conviction — corroborates, rather
  than undercuts, EHanson3's point that the disclosed text cites the wrong
  (non-criminal) predicate instrument. No source found connecting this to
  his VVA lobbying disclosures.
- **GAO's own numbers, read straight, are worth flagging for the write-up:**
  the 2026 report (audit window: 2024 Q3/Q4 + 2025 Q1/Q2) sampled 247–258
  lobbyists and found zero with disclosable convictions — consistent across
  the last several years' reports (2019–2025 series, all fetched in search
  results). This is corroborating context for E-GAO's base-rate-artifact
  argument (the sample is structurally unlikely to ever contain a known-
  convicted lobbyist, given ~18 in the whole corpus vs. tens of thousands of
  filings), not a contradiction of it.

Component-level surfacing-time novelty-lite (unchanged, folded in above):
