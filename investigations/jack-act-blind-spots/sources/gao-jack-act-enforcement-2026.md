# GAO 2025 Lobbying Disclosure compliance report (JACK Act enforcement)

- **What:** GAO-26-108486, "2025 LOBBYING DISCLOSURE: Observations on Compliance
  with Requirements" — 19th annual statutory review of LDA compliance.
- **Source:** https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108486.pdf (published 2026-06-30).
  Copy saved as `sources/gao-26-108486-2025-lobbying-disclosure.pdf`, fetched
  2026-07-07.
- **Used by:** the conviction-disclosure vein (Hunter, Hanson) — establishes the
  enforcement-mechanism context. Outside data, disclosed per challenge rules;
  legal/context standard only, never evidence for a filing-level claim about any
  named lobbyist.
- **Cross-checked against 2019-2024:** see
  `gao-jack-act-enforcement-2019-2024-series.md` — the same methodology and
  the same zero-disclosed-convictions result hold in all six prior annual
  reports, i.e. every year since the JACK Act took effect, not just this one.

## Key operative points (quotes)

1. **Zero JACK Act enforcement, ever:** "USAO officials did not report taking any
   civil or criminal enforcement actions for LDA violations against lobbyists in
   2025. Similarly, USAO officials stated that, as of December 2025, the
   Department of Justice has not brought any prosecutions related to
   nondisclosure of relevant crimes under the JACK Act since the law's
   enactment." (p.9) — law effective 2019-01-03.
2. **The JACK Act compliance check ran backwards from what "compliance" implies:**
   GAO did not audit known-convicted lobbyists' disclosure behavior. It took its
   general stratified LD-2 sample (247 individual lobbyists, drawn for unrelated
   compliance metrics) and ran criminal-background checks (Accurint/CLEAR
   databases) on all of them. Result: "none of the 247 individual lobbyists
   disclosed convictions reportable under the [JACK Act]... we confirmed that the
   lobbyists did not appear to have relevant convictions to disclose and
   therefore we did not identify any violations." (Enclosure IV, p.28) — i.e., GAO
   found a clean population, not clean behavior; the sample is not conditioned on
   having a conviction at all.
3. **The enforcement mechanism that does exist (USAO referrals, 12,391 since
   2016, ~46% resolved) is a failure-to-file trigger** — the Secretary of the
   Senate/Clerk of the House refer lobbyists who never submitted an LD-2/LD-203.
   It has no described mechanism for catching a timely-filed report that
   silently omits one required field (the conviction-disclosure line). Nothing
   in the report suggests this system would surface a Hunter- or Hanson-type gap.
4. No mention of Duncan Hunter, Harold Hanson, or any convicted lobbyist by name
   — the report is population-level only.

## Caveats for case use

- This is a **structural/systemic** fact (enforcement mechanism design and
  history), not evidence about any individual filer — cite it for "why would
  this go uncaught," never for "GAO reviewed and cleared/missed Hunter/Hanson."
- The 247-lobbyist sample and the 0-convictions-found result should NOT be read
  as "GAO checked compliance and it's fine" in any story text — that
  mischaracterizes a base-rate artifact as a compliance finding. The corpus's
  own convicted-lobbyist population is small (~18 in `derived_convicted_lobbyist_register`)
  for the same reason: predicate convictions among registered lobbyists are rare,
  so a random sample of average size is expected to miss them entirely.
