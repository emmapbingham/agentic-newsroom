# JACK Act guidance notice (legal basis for conviction-disclosure screens)

- **What:** Joint guidance letter, "Dear Lobbying Registrant," March 29, 2019 —
  House Legislative Resource Center + Senate Office of Public Records.
- **Source:** https://www.senate.gov/legislative/resources/pdf/Notice_re_JACK_Act.pdf
  (copy saved as `sources/jack-act-notice-2019-03-29.pdf`, fetched 2026-07-06).
- **Used by:** the `conviction-quarterly-gaps` screen and the data-vs-law
  conviction-disclosure vein (leads 2, 59, 60). Outside data, disclosed per
  challenge rules; used for the legal standard only, never as evidence of any
  factual claim about a filer.

Key operative points (quotes from the notice):

1. The JACK Act (P.L. 115-418, effective **2019-01-03**) amended the LDA "to
   require **all registrations (LD-1) and all quarterly activity reports
   (LD-2)** to include: 'for any listed lobbyist who was convicted in a Federal
   or State court of an offense involving bribery, extortion, embezzlement, an
   illegal kickback, tax evasion, fraud, a conflict of interest, making a false
   statement, perjury, or money laundering, the date of the conviction and a
   description of the offense.'" (LD-2 line 29; LD-1 line 15.)
2. **No lookback limit**: registrants must list "every listed lobbyist with
   **any prior conviction**" of a predicate offense — the notice's own worked
   example uses a 1/1/1970 conviction.
3. **Every filing, forever**: "Once a JACK Act disclosure is required for a
   listed lobbyist, that disclosure will be required on **every future
   registration or quarterly report that includes that lobbyist**."
4. Sanction language: "making false statements on LDA filings, or
   **intentionally omitting required information** from LDA filings, may
   constitute a violation of federal law." (LDA §7 / 2 U.S.C. §1606: civil up
   to $200,000 per violation for knowing violations after notice; knowing and
   corrupt → criminal, up to 5 years.)
5. Caveats for case use: whether a given conviction "involves" a predicate is
   a legal judgment the offices won't opine on ("filers may wish to consult
   with private counsel"); the notice is silent on **pardons**. Strongest
   in-data workaround: when the registrant itself disclosed the conviction on
   at least one filing, the registrant's own predicate judgment is on record.
