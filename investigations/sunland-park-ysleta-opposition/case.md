# A New Mexico casino has quietly opposed a Texas tribal-gaming bill for four years

- **slug:** sunland-park-ysleta-opposition
- **status:** closed
- **confidence:** medium
- **coverage:** under-reported
- **opened:** 2026-07-01   **last updated:** 2026-07-02

## Hypothesis
Sunland Park Racetrack & Casino (New Mexico) is the only identified filer with
explicit, sustained lobbying opposition (2022 Q3 – 2025 Q4, one explained
pause — a formal LDA termination and re-registration spanning 2023 Q1, see
E6 in evidence.md) to
H.R.2208/S.4196 and its successor H.R.2873/S.1536 (Tribal Gaming Regulatory
Compliance Act), which would let the Ysleta del Sur Pueblo and Alabama-Coushatta
Tribe of Texas operate gaming under standard federal law (IGRA) instead of a
more restrictive 1987 statute — and this specific opposition has drawn no
identified press coverage. **Revised 2026-07-02 after E8 (novelty scan):** the
narrower, still-novel claim is that Sunland Park's role has never been
reported, not that the bill's repeated stalling itself is unexplained — a
different, already-reported opponent (Sen. Cornyn / Texas state government,
2019–2020 coverage) is the on-record reason the Senate side has never
advanced any version of this bill, predating and independent of Sunland
Park. See E8 for full detail.

## Why it's newsworthy
**Revised 2026-07-02 after E8.** The original framing — "if Sunland Park's
opposition is a material reason the bill keeps stalling, this is a clean
market-entry-blocking story" — is weakened by E8: the Senate-side stall
already has a named, on-record, previously-reported explanation (Cornyn/Texas
state government's 2019 objections) that predates and doesn't depend on
Sunland Park. Leaning on "Sunland Park may be why this keeps failing" risks
recycling an already-solved mystery under a new, unproven suspect.

The more defensible newsworthy angle is an **update, not an unmasking**: this
fight's last press coverage is from 2019–2020, when Cornyn and Texas state
officials were the visible opposition to the *first* bill (H.R.759). Six
years, one more House passage (2021), and three more bill reintroductions
later, nobody has reported that (a) the tribes are still trying, or (b) a
second, quieter, never-named opponent — Sunland Park Racetrack & Casino, just
across the state line from one of the reservations — has paid a lobbyist
every quarter since 2022 specifically to keep this bill dead, citing fear of
a future Class III casino competitor, and did so continuously through a full
change of its own corporate ownership (E7). That opposition is also the
latest chapter of a 15+ year pattern for this specific casino (E8) — it
fought two *other* tribal casino proposals the same way in 2008–2011 — so the
story is "an old, institutional actor is still doing what it's always done,
now via continuous federal lobbying disclosure, and nobody's noticed" rather
than "we caught someone new." Still a real gap in coverage; just a narrower
and more honest one than the original framing.

**Added timeliness (2026-07-02, E9):** the original 2019 political blocker
is itself now a live, dateable news thread. Sen. Cornyn — who asked Senate
Indian Affairs to hold off on the first version of this bill in 2019 — is
leaving the Senate, having lost the May 2026 Texas GOP primary runoff to
**Ken Paxton**. Paxton was one of the three Texas officials cited in that
2019 objection, and has since, as sitting AG, personally led litigation
against Ysleta del Sur's Speaking Rock casino, winning a court ruling
against the tribe. He's now the Republican nominee for Cornyn's seat,
facing Talarico in November 2026. That means this story can be pegged to a
live election: the seat that once housed a comparatively soft "hold on
state concerns" position may soon belong to the tribes' single most
aggressive, personal legal adversary — while, underneath that headline
fight, Sunland Park has quietly worked the same side of the issue through
federal lobbying disclosures the whole time. That combination — a live
electoral hook plus an unreported quiet actor — is what makes this
publishable now rather than at some other time.

## What would confirm it / what would kill it
- **Confirms:** The bill has had real momentum at points (a hearing, a markup,
  inclusion in an early draft of a must-pass vehicle that later got stripped)
  that plausibly correlates with Sunland Park's lobbying activity; Sunland
  Park's spend/registrant activity is not simply generic "monitor everything"
  boilerplate but specifically timed around key legislative moments.
  **Borne out by E5 (confirmed against congress.gov's own action log):** the
  bill passed the House twice (2019, 2021) with no recorded opposition, the
  second time without even a formal markup, then stalled at bare committee
  referral every Congress since Sunland Park's opposition began (2022 Q3) —
  real momentum existed and stopped, which is a stronger fact pattern than
  "never had momentum." This is confirmed timing correlation, not proof of
  mechanism.
- **Refutes (the boring_explanation):** The bill has never had any committee
  action or floor movement in any Congress since 2022, regardless of who was
  lobbying for or against it — i.e., low legislative priority for a narrow,
  two-tribe fix with no natural champion is a sufficient explanation on its
  own, and Sunland Park's opposition is boilerplate competitive monitoring
  that isn't actually the operative cause of the stall. **Not fully supported
  by E5** on the House side (it did move, twice, before 2022) but **plausible
  on the Senate side** — S.4196/S.1536/S.2564 have never had committee action
  in any Congress on record, which could reflect Senate Indian Affairs'
  general throughput on this class of bill rather than anything
  Sunland-Park-specific. Not yet checked against a base-rate comparison set.

## Verdict
**Closed, supported, confidence medium.** Sunland Park Racetrack & Casino is
the sole, sustained (14 of 15 quarters, 2022 Q3–2025 Q4, one explained
registration-lapse pause) opponent of H.R.3723/S.2564 and its predecessors,
independently corroborated in both Senate and House disclosure regimes,
specifically and consistently motivated (fear of a future Class III
competitor), surviving a full change of corporate ownership, and unreported
anywhere — confirmed via a builder→skeptic→judge pass (E10) including a
corrected base-rate check showing sustained solo opposition is genuinely
rare even within the tribal-gaming lobbying niche specifically (Sunland
Park's streak is ~4.5x the next-longest). Not claimed as the cause of the
bill's stall: a separate, already-reported, Senate-specific explanation
(Cornyn/Texas state government, 2019–2020) predates and is independent of
Sunland Park (E8/E9) — this case's claim is scoped to "who's quietly
opposing it and has never been named," not "why does it stall." Confidence
held at medium rather than high because the underlying claim rests on
self-reported LDA disclosure (not independently corroborated by a public
Sunland Park statement) and on a bounded, not exhaustive, novelty scan.
All identified pre-publication items are resolved: E7's deal-value figures
are reconciled against primary SEC/PR sourcing, Paxton has made no direct
statement on the current bill (re-checked, confirmed current as of
2026-07-02), the 2023 Q1 filing gap is a routine, corpus-common LDA
registration termination/re-registration (not a missed filing, not
distinctive to Sunland Park), and the bill has been confirmed via its
actual text to have no fee-to-trust component (E11) — decoupling the
`localgov-fee-to-trust-monitoring` lead as a genuinely separate,
unpursued thread rather than a dependency of this case. Ready for
publication pending only the standing Paxton legal-risk caution in
Sources/legal-risk notes (re-check immediately before anything ships,
given his active Senate candidacy).
