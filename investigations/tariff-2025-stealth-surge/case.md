## Hypothesis

In 2025, tariff (TAR) lobbying surged in a way unique among all ~80 LDA issue
codes — driven by **both** a flood of first-time registrants (211 firms whose
first TAR activity is 2025) **and** dramatic scale-ups by incumbents (Ballard
Partners 11→82 TAR activities) — a lobbying market forming in real time around
the new administration's tariff agenda.

## Why it's newsworthy

Tariffs were the defining economic-policy fight of 2025; this is the influence
industry mobilizing around it, measurable at firm/client granularity from public
filings. The corpus's quarterly grain lets us show *when* the money moved and
*who* moved first — and that the entrant rush is tariff-specific, not a generic
post-election bump (the next-most-rushed issue drew +4 new entrants vs tariff's
+152). Every firm, client, and filing is nameable and sourced.

**Novelty reframe (2026-06-14):** the headline surge is *well-covered* (see Prior
coverage). Our distinct, defensible contribution is now twofold: (1) a
**verification / precision exhibit** — the pipeline independently reproduced the
published analysis from the same primary records; and (2) the **under-reported
seam** below the coverage — the foreign-owned/foreign-association cohort, the
genuinely-new-vs-incumbent decomposition, and a *quantified* relief-vs-protection
split. We do **not** go all-in on the headline "tariff lobbying surged" claim
(others reported it first); we own the records-level granularity beneath it.

## Prior coverage (novelty-scan, 2026-06-14)

**Verdict: `well-covered` core, `under-reported` seam.** The 2025 tariff-lobbying
surge is extensively reported — including by the outlets that run the *same*
LDA-filings analysis we do, which is the worst case for novelty and the best case
for validation:

- **OpenSecrets / LegiStorm:** tariff-related lobbying rose from ~120
  organizations (2024) to ~382 (2025); firms reporting tariff-*bill* work climbed
  59→231; tariff-bill lobbying revenue ~$2.1M→$14.6M (~7×); **Ballard Partners
  ~$3.8M, >¼ of all tariff lobbying revenue** — singling out the same firm our
  E4/E6 do, independently.
- **Bloomberg Government / Reason / AOL:** the relief/exemption fight (pharma,
  autos, energy, semiconductors exempted; the "America First" relief pitch;
  Apple's $100B pledge) — covered at the *outcome*/strategy level.

**The under-reported seam (our remaining novel contribution):** the coverage
centers on *domestic* industries and their US trade associations (PhRMA, AAPC,
API). Our E8–E9 cohort centers on **foreign-owned manufacturers and direct
foreign trade associations** (Schaeffler/Tetra Pak/Wacker/Alpek; UNICA/IBA
Brazil, Orica Australia, DigitalEurope) entering US lobbying — a "foreign
industries lobbying Washington" angle the coverage does not emphasize. Also
un-mined at our granularity: the **genuinely-new vs. incumbent-adding-a-code
decomposition** (E2: ~126 of 211 truly new) and a **quantified
relief-vs-protection split** across the full cohort (the pending Haiku enrichment
— coverage asserts "mostly relief" anecdotally; nobody has measured it).

*Provenance note:* these are outside-reporting citations — a **fenced provenance
class** that corroborates direction and is **not** evidence for any claim in this
case; every claim still rests on the cited LDA filings. Coverage is
**contemporaneous** with the corpus window (2025), so **no lead-time claim is
available** (this retires the "lead-lag vs press" owed item below).

## What would confirm it / what would kill it

- **Confirms:** TAR new-entrant count and activity volume spike sharply in 2025,
  far above the issue's own history and above all other issues; named firms show
  the surge in their own filings; the income series rises in the same quarters.
- **Refutes / innocent accounts to clear:**
  - *Left-censoring artifact* — "first-time in 2025" could just mean "first time
    in our 2022– window." (Mitigated: 2022 is the inflated baseline year, so the
    +152 excess is conservative; but verify a sample of "first-timers" weren't
    TAR-active pre-2022 via lda.senate.gov history.)
  - *Reclassification* — pre-2025 tariff work filed under TRD (Trade) recoded to
    TAR in 2025. Must check TRD trend alongside TAR.
  - *Generic transition bump* — every issue rises post-election. (Mitigated by
    run 7: TAR is a ~38× outlier; no other issue rushes. Still, confirm income
    surge isn't corpus-wide.)
  - *Duplicate/amendment inflation* — handled: panels are deduped (canonical
    latest-posted filing per engagement).
